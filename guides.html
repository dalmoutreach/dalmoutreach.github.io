Make the text bigger

About 2 minutes · Makes everything on your phone easier to read

  1. Open the Settings app (the gray gear).
  2. Tap Display & Brightness, then Text Size.
  3. Drag the slider to the right until the words are comfortable to read.
  4. Go back one screen and turn on Bold Text to make letters darker.
  1. Open the Settings app (the gear).
  2. Tap Display.
  3. Tap Font size. On some phones, like Samsung, it's called Font size and style.
  4. Drag the slider to the right until the words are comfortable to read.

Need it bigger still? On iPhone, go to Settings, then Accessibility, then Display & Text Size, then Larger Text. On Android, look for Display size or Screen zoom on the same screen as Font size.

Make a video call

About 3 minutes · See family and friends face to face

With FaceTime (to another iPhone or iPad)

  1. Open the green FaceTime app.
  2. Tap New FaceTime.
  3. Type the person's name or phone number, then tap their name.
  4. Tap the green FaceTime button with the camera picture.
  5. To hang up, tap the red button with the X.

With WhatsApp (works on any phone)

  1. Open WhatsApp (the green speech bubble).
  2. Tap the chat with the person you want to see.
  3. Tap the video camera picture at the top of the screen.
  4. Wait for them to answer. Hold the phone at arm's length so they can see your face.
  5. To hang up, tap the red button.

Sit facing a window or lamp so the light is on your face, not behind you. It helps them see you clearly.

Send a photo by text message

About 2 minutes · Share pictures with the people you love

  1. Open the green Messages app.
  2. Tap the conversation with the person you want to send it to.
  3. Tap the + button to the left of the typing box, then tap Photos.
  4. Tap the photo you want to send. It appears in the typing box.
  5. Tap the blue arrow to send it.
  1. Open your Messages app.
  2. Tap the conversation with the person you want to send it to.
  3. Tap the + or the picture icon next to the typing box.
  4. Tap the photo you want to send.
  5. Tap the send arrow.

You can also send a photo straight from your Photos or Gallery app: open the photo, tap the Share button, then choose Messages.

Connect to Wi-Fi

About 3 minutes · Use the internet at home without using up your data

Before you start

Find your Wi-Fi name and password. They're usually printed on a sticker on your internet box (the router).

  1. Open Settings and tap Wi-Fi.
  2. Make sure the Wi-Fi switch is on (green).
  3. Tap your Wi-Fi name in the list.
  4. Type the password exactly, including capital letters, then tap Join.
  5. A check mark next to the name means you're connected.
  1. Open Settings, then tap Network & internet or Connections.
  2. Tap Wi-Fi and make sure it's turned on.
  3. Tap your Wi-Fi name in the list.
  4. Type the password exactly, including capital letters, then tap Connect.
  5. The word "Connected" under the name means you're on.

Be careful on free public Wi-Fi (at a café or library). It's fine for reading, but wait until you're home to do banking.

How to spot a scam

The most important guide on this page · Scammers target good, trusting people

Common tricks

  • "Grandma, I'm in trouble." A caller says they're a grandchild in jail or the hospital and needs money fast and in secret.
  • Government threats. Someone says they're from Social Security, Medicare, or the IRS and that you'll be arrested or lose benefits.
  • Computer pop-ups. A loud warning says your computer has a virus and to call a number.
  • Shut-off threats. A caller says your power will be cut off today unless you pay right now.
  • Prizes and romance. You "won" something but must pay a fee, or a new online friend suddenly needs money.

Remember: Stop, hang up, check

Real banks, government offices, and companies will never make you pay with gift cards, wire transfers, cryptocurrency, or a payment app, and will never rush you or ask you to keep it secret. Hang up, then call back using the number on your card, bill, or statement.

Passwords made simple

Keep your accounts safe without the headache

  1. Use three or four random words. Something like "teapot river purple garden" is long, strong, and easy to remember.
  2. Use a different password for email and banking. If one gets out, the others stay safe.
  3. Writing them down is okay, in a notebook kept somewhere safe at home. Never on a sticky note on the computer.
  4. Turn on two-step verification for email and banking. It texts you a code when someone signs in.
  5. Never read a code out to anyone. If someone calls asking for the code that was just texted to you, it's a scam. Hang up.

Our volunteers can sit with you and set up a password notebook or two-step verification. Ask for help. We'll never ask to know your passwords.

Get ready for a video doctor visit

Telehealth lets you see your doctor from home

  1. Find the link. Your doctor's office will send it by email, text, or in your patient portal. Ask them if you can't find it.
  2. Charge your device and connect to Wi-Fi if you can.
  3. Get set up 15 minutes early. Tap the link. If it asks to use your camera and microphone, tap Allow.
  4. Pick a quiet, bright spot. Prop the phone or tablet up so your hands are free.
  5. Have these nearby: your list of medicines, glasses, a pen and paper, and your questions written down.

Ask a volunteer or family member to do a practice call with you the day before. It makes the real visit much calmer.

If you think you've been scammed

It happens to smart, careful people. Act quickly, and don't be ashamed.

  1. Call your bank or card company right away using the number on the back of your card. Ask them to stop any payments.
  2. Gift cards? Call the company on the back of the card and tell them it was used in a scam.
  3. Change your passwords if you gave any out, starting with email and banking.
  4. Gave someone access to your computer? Turn it off and have a trusted person check it before you use it again.
  5. Report it. Call the AARP Fraud Watch Helpline at 877-908-3360 for free support, and report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

More numbers that can help are on our Helpful numbers page. And you can always ask us to sit with you while you make the calls.
